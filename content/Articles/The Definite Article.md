The Pidgin language has one definite article: "the." It is used with both proper and improper nouns under certain conditions.

This article can placed before common nouns when the noun being referred to is known. Two sample sentences to demonstrate the use and purpose of of definite articles with common nouns in Pidgin are:
1. Show me the pesin wey bring you. (Show me the person who brought you here.)
2. The house she build for her village dey fine. (The house she built in her village is beautiful.)
3. Show me the road wey dey go Aba. (Show me the road that leads to Aba.)

In Pidgin, the is only used with proper nouns for titles. This means that the proper noun "the" must to be used with the noun forms illustrated in the following sentences:
1. I sabi the President of Nigeria. (I know the president of Nigeria)
2. Ikeja na the Capital of Lagos. (Ikeja is the capital of Lagos.)

But the definite articles is not to be used with all other proper nouns. Here are some examples to demonstrate scenarios where a definite article is required in English but not in Pidgin:
1. The match na for National Stadium. (The game is at the National Stadium.)
2. They do parade for Government House. (There was a parade at the Government House)

In general, definite articles are not used before names (proper or common), except when one wishes to mention that they are referring to something that had been discussed in the past. Here are some example sentences where the definite articles is not needed (but is needed in pidgin):
1. They go market. (They went to the market.)
2. My papa go bank. (My father went to the bank.)
